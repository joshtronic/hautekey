Click a key to try it. On a Mac, tap your own right ⌘ too.

One key, one place, do it all.

⌘
Tap it to open Spotlight, or Raycast, Alfred, any app, or a shortcut you pick.
⌘+1…0
Open or switch to an app. The first time you run it, the numbers follow the apps pinned in your Dock.
⌘+W
Maximize the front window.
⌘+AD
Snap it to exactly the left or right half. No gaps, no 48%.
⌘+S
Restore it to where it was before you snapped it.

Any key from 0–9 or A–Z can open an app or snap a window. The Hyper key can be right ⌘, right ⌥, right ⌃, or Caps Lock, and Caps Lock can double as Control. Your setup is one plain file you can keep in your dotfiles.

Ten years of chasing GNOME's Super key.

On GNOME, tapping Super opens search and holding it moves you around your apps and windows. I wanted the same thing on my Mac, on the right-hand key, where my thumb already is.

  1. 2014

    Alfred for launching, Spectacle for windows. Tools of the trade

  2. 2016

    Hammerspoon, then Karabiner: tapping right ⌘ opens the launcher, and right ⌘ plus a number opens an app. The right command key

  3. 2016

    A macOS update broke Karabiner, and the "third-party hackery" sent me back to Linux. Switching back

  4. 2024

    Back on a Mac with Karabiner plus Raycast: right ⌘ for the launcher, numbers for apps, WASD for windows. Aligning hotkeys

It worked, but it took two apps, and then window snapping went behind a subscription. Haute Key is that whole setup in one app.

Free, and staying that way.

Haute Key is open source under the GPL. There's no trial, no license key, and nothing to unlock. If it saves you a subscription, you can say thanks with a sponsorship.
