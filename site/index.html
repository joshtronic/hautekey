The kits

Everything Haute Key does starts from one key. Here's what comes on it, out of the box.

Base kit

Tap the Hyper key to search. Hold it with a number to open or switch to an app. On first launch, the numbers follow the apps pinned in your Dock.

⌘ 123450
⌘ tap
Spotlight, or Raycast, Alfred, any app, or a shortcut you choose
⌘ + 1–9, 0
Open the app on that key, or bring it to the front

Window kit

Exact halves of the screen, no gaps and no 48%. Restore puts a window back where it was before you snapped it.

⌘ + W
Maximize
⌘ + A
Left half
⌘ + S
Restore
⌘ + D
Right half

Mods

Pick the key that becomes Hyper, turn Caps Lock into Control, and bind any key from 0–9 or A–Z to an app or a snap.

Specs

The details, before you install anything.

Runs onmacOS 15 or later, Apple silicon and Intel
Built withSwift, as a native menu bar app. No kernel extension, no background daemon.
PermissionsAccessibility, to move windows. Input Monitoring, to see the Hyper key. The app walks you through both.
SettingsOne plain file at ~/.config/hautekey/config.json, edited in the app or by hand. Symlink it into your dotfiles; changes apply live.
LicenseGPL-3.0. The source is on GitHub.

Designer's notes

I bounce between GNOME and macOS, and GNOME gets one thing exactly right: the Super key. Tap it and you're searching. Hold it and press a number to jump to an app, or a direction to snap the window. I map it to the right-hand key, where my thumb already is.

I've chased that same muscle memory on a Mac for over a decade, through Alfred, Spectacle, Hammerspoon, Karabiner, and Raycast. The last setup worked, but it took two apps, and then window snapping went behind a subscription.

Haute Key is that whole setup in one small app: the key, the launcher tap, the app numbers, and the window snaps. It's what I use every day.
